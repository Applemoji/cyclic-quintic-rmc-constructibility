# Geometry audit of the cyclic-quintic RMC proof

**Completed: 16 October 2026, São Paulo time.**

**Verdict: the encoded construction rules are sound for the standard idealized restricted marked-ruler-and-compass (RMC) model, relative to the input field. No extra geometric power is needed to interpret the checked finite-constructibility theorem.**

This is a written mathematical audit of the bridge from the Lean coordinate definition to geometric constructions. It is not a newly formalized Euclidean-geometry development, an external referee report, or a novelty assessment. The previously successful Lean check establishes the coordinate theorem; the argument below supplies the geometric interpretation.

Audited source: `CyclicQuinticRMC_SET_FIX_V2.lean`, 3,075 lines, 99,015 bytes.

SHA-256: `db36d978c9c88796885ebcbed464310d5ad2c69c3ff6208e2902b5a7eb5bf9e7`.

The intended geometric model is the finite-point-sequence definition in Elliot Benjamin and C. Snyder, *Some fifth roots that are constructible by marked ruler and compass*, Rocky Mountain Journal of Mathematics 46(3), 2016, pp. 749–767, §2, especially pp. 750–751. DOI: [10.1216/RMJ-2016-46-3-749](https://doi.org/10.1216/RMJ-2016-46-3-749). The [author-uploaded full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/307913235_Some_fifth_roots_that_are_constructible_by_marked_ruler_and_compass) was consulted.

That definition allows ordinary line/circle constructions and verging through a previously constructed point between two lines or between a line and a circle, using two marks one unit apart. It does not require the fixed point to lie between the marks. We use only the line–circle verging operation, together with ordinary straightedge-and-compass constructions. Thus soundness suffices: our encoded rules need not represent every available RMC operation.

For a real subfield K, take (0,0), (1,0), and the available points (k,0), k in K, as starting data. Every individual construction below uses only finitely many such points. When K is generated by finitely many supplied coefficients over Q, each needed element of K can itself be obtained by finitely many field operations on those coefficients.

## 2. The bridge theorem

Let G(K) be the set of real t for which (t,0) is obtainable by a finite geometric RMC construction from the starting data above. Then

\[
\operatorname{Constructible}(K,t)\ \Longrightarrow\ t\in G(K).
\]

**Proof.** Use induction on the derivation of `Constructible K t`. Its eight constructors are exhausted in §§3–4. Each constructor is replaced by a finite permitted geometric construction. Concatenating the finitely many prerequisite constructions and then the new construction remains finite. Reusing already constructed points is allowed. This proves the implication. In particular, the inductive definition is a finite-construction definition; passing through a closure theorem does not introduce an infinite construction.

## 3. Ordinary arithmetic and square roots

The oriented coordinate axes, perpendiculars, parallels, midpoints, and quarter-turns can be constructed with the ordinary straightedge and compass from the unit segment. The following are their usual finite arithmetic constructions; they do not use verging.

| Lean constructor | Geometric realization | Exceptional case |
|---|---|---|
| `base` | Use the supplied point (t,0), t in K. | No new operation. |
| `add` | Translate the directed length of one axis segment to the endpoint of the other, using compass transfer and the appropriate orientation. | A zero summand reuses the other point. |
| `neg` | Reflect (t,0) across the origin. | Zero is already available. |
| `mul` | Construct (1,y). The line through the origin and (1,y) meets the vertical line through (x,0) at (x,xy). Project and transfer the second coordinate to the horizontal axis. | The line through (0,0),(1,y) is never vertical, so x=0 or y=0 causes no failure. |
| `inv` | For x nonzero, the line through the origin and (x,1) meets the vertical line with first coordinate 1 at (1,1/x). Extract its second coordinate. | Lean defines the inverse of zero to be zero; this branch simply reuses the origin. No geometric division by zero is required. |
| `sqrt` | For t positive, draw the circle with diameter from (-1,0) to (t,0). Its upper intersection with the vertical axis is (0,sqrt(t)); transfer this length to the horizontal axis. | At t=0 reuse the origin. The constructor excludes t<0. |

Constructing (s,t) from axis points is an ordinary perpendicular-line intersection. Conversely, from a constructed point (s,t), drop perpendiculars to the axes and use a quarter-turn to obtain both (s,0) and (t,0), with their signs. This explains why either coordinate of a verging output may be used in later scalar operations.

All circles used in the nonzero cases have positive radius and can be specified by an already constructed center and a distinct already constructed point on the circumference. All lines can be specified by distinct constructed points. Zero cases reuse existing points rather than requesting degenerate circles or undefined lines.

## 4. The two verging constructors

Suppose the premises of `vergeX` or `vergeY` hold. The already available scalar inputs are a,b,c,r, with a nonzero and r positive. Construct

\[
O=(0,0),\qquad \ell=\{(s,t):s=a\},\qquad
\Gamma=\{(s,t):(s-b)^2+(t-c)^2=r^2\}.
\]

The line is determined by (a,0) and (a,1), which are distinct. The circle has center (b,c) and passes through (b+r,c), a distinct constructed point because r>0. The fixed point O does not lie on the line because a is nonzero.

Write the two ruler-mark positions as P=(X,Y), Q=(V,W). The Lean hypotheses are exactly

\[
V=a,\qquad (X-b)^2+(Y-c)^2=r^2,\qquad
XW=YV,\qquad (X-V)^2+(Y-W)^2=1.
\]

They say that Q lies on the line, P lies on the circle, O,P,Q are collinear, and the marks have distance one. In detail, Q is nonzero because V=a is nonzero. If P is nonzero, the determinant equation gives a unique common line through O and Q containing P. The distance equation makes P and Q distinct and, since distances are nonnegative, fixes their Euclidean distance at +1, not merely at a signed value whose interpretation is ambiguous. The marked straightedge can therefore occupy precisely this permitted line–circle verging placement. P is the primary point, so its coordinates are available by the ordinary projections described above.

There is one harmless degenerate case: P may equal O in the abstract constructor. Both scalar outputs are then zero, already available. We do not need to count this case as a new verging operation. It cannot create a nonconstructible coordinate.

Neither W nor the unknown coordinates X,Y must be available before the operation: they are output coordinates of the placement. Requiring them as inputs would remove the very operation the RMC definition permits. Only a,b,c,r and O are inputs.

The two constructors express the two possible coordinate projections of the same kind of permitted point. Their use does not authorize an additional simultaneous geometric constraint, circle–circle verging, a movable fixed point, or a variable distance between marks.

**Consequently both verging constructors preserve membership in G(K), completing the bridge theorem.**

## 5. The sextic reconstruction actually supplies such a placement

The theorem `sextic_root_mem` starts with available v2,v3,v4,h, with h nonzero. Set

\[
m=2-2v_2-v_3,\quad q=\frac{v_3-m-4h}{8},\quad
A=\frac{v_4-(h+m/2)^2+2h+4q}{4q},\quad
R=h+\frac{m^2}{16A}+q.
\]

Its strict hypotheses are q>0, A>0, R>0. Therefore

\[
a=\sqrt A>0,\qquad c=\sqrt q>0,\qquad
b=\frac{m}{4a},\qquad r=\sqrt R>0
\]

are available using field operations and three nonnegative square-root constructions. Every displayed denominator is nonzero. In particular, a,c are nonzero, and

\[
4ab=m,\qquad r^2=b^2+c^2+h.
\]

The coefficient relation v5=-mh-2h² makes the sextic f satisfy the identity

\[
f(z)=z^6-2z^5+v_2z^4+v_3z^3+v_4z^2+v_5z+h^2
=\big((z^2-h)(z-1)-2abz\big)^2
+4a^2c^2z^2-4c^2z^2(z-1)^2.
\]

For any real root z, the exceptional values are excluded: f(0)=h²>0 and f(1)=4a²(b²+c²)>0. Define, only as existence witnesses,

\[
X=\frac{az}{z-1},\qquad
Y=\frac{z^2-h-2bX}{2c},\qquad
P=(X,Y),\qquad Q=\frac{z-1}{z}P.
\]

The identity gives X²+Y²=z². It follows directly that

\[
Q_x=a,\qquad (X-b)^2+(Y-c)^2=r^2,\qquad
\det(P,Q)=0,\qquad |P-Q|^2=1.
\]

These are the actual constructor premises, rather than just a necessary eliminated polynomial equation. In particular, no extraneous root introduced by squaring is being accepted without a geometric witness. Also X is nonzero, so P is not the harmless origin case above. Finally

\[
X-a=\frac a{z-1}\ne0,\qquad z=\frac X{X-a}.
\]

Once the permitted placement supplies P, field operations recover z. Negative z is allowed: the definition permits the ruler line on either side of the marks and imposes no betweenness requirement on O.

Using z in the formulas for P,Q is a proof that the required placement exists. The line and circle themselves depend only on the already available coefficients, not on z. Thus the argument does not construct the line or circle from the unknown root and then use that geometry to recover it.

In `cyclic_quintic_closed`, membership of the auxiliary cubic coefficients and all the sextic parameters is proved by descent to the base field and subsequent field operations. The unknown quintic conjugates occur in the existence proof and algebraic identities, not as `Constructible.base` inputs.

## 6. The auxiliary cubic uses the same permitted geometry

The script does not assume a cubic-root constructor. It proves cubic constructibility with ordinary operations and another line–circle placement.

For a depressed cubic u³+pu+q=0, first handle q=0 using u=0 or u²=-p and signed square-root extraction. If p is nonzero and q is nonzero, scale by a positive integer N to obtain v=u/N, p'=p/N², q'=q/N³ with |v|<1 and p'>-1/2. Put

\[
a=\frac{2q'}{p'},\quad b=-\frac a{p'},\quad
h=1+\frac{2a^2}{p'},\quad r=\sqrt{b^2+h},\quad X=v+a.
\]

Here a is nonzero, and

\[
b^2+h=1+\frac{a^2(1+2p')}{p'^2}>0.
\]

The line and circle parameters are therefore available before verging. If X=0, recover u=-Na by field operations. Otherwise v is nonzero, and the real witness

\[
Y=\sqrt{X^2(1/v^2-1)}
\]

exists because |v|<1. The identity

\[
v^2(h+2bX)-X^2=\frac{-2a}{p'}(v^3+p'v+q')=0
\]

shows that P=(X,Y) lies on the circle centered at (b,0) of radius r. With Q=(a/X)P, the line, collinearity and unit-distance constraints all hold. The operation supplies X and then u=N(X-a).

The witness Y need not be constructible before verging. Its square-root expression is used to establish existence of a real placement, not as an extra permitted constructor with an unavailable radicand. A circle center with second coordinate zero is legal in the underlying RMC definition. The condition c>0 in the separate sextic reconstruction is a condition for that reconstruction formula, not a restriction on all permissible circles.

The integer N in the code is chosen using u in its existence proof. This is legitimate for an existential constructibility theorem. Even this dependence can be removed: every real root of the depressed cubic obeys |u|≤1+|p|+|q|. For |u|>1, the equation implies |u|³≤|p||u|+|q|≤(|p|+|q|)|u|. Thus choosing N>4+2|p|+|q| already gives the bounds used in the proof using coefficient data alone. No root-location oracle is needed for the geometric input.

If p=0 and q is nonzero, the code uses y=u²+u. It satisfies

\[
y^3+3qy+(q-q^2)=0,\qquad
u=\frac{y-q}{y+1},\qquad
y+1=(u+1/2)^2+3/4>0.
\]

The auxiliary equation has nonzero linear coefficient and is handled by the preceding construction. If p=q=0, u=0. A general cubic is reduced by a field-operation translation. Thus all cubic branches ultimately use only the audited primitive operations.

## 7. Selection, finiteness and the meaning of the result

The geometric constructibility statement is existential: a permitted placement producing the desired point can be selected. This is the finite-point-sequence meaning of construction being used, just as a particular intersection point can be chosen in an ordinary compass construction. The code does not assert that an arbitrary placement returned by a device must be the desired root, nor supply a practical numerical branch-selection algorithm.

The origin and the distance between the marks stay fixed throughout each operation. All placement parameters are constructed first. Tangencies, sign choices and a ruler extending beyond its two marks are not excluded by the stated idealized RMC definition. No additional transversality or finite-precision manufacturing rule is being assumed. Such requirements would describe a different problem.

The inductive derivation uses finitely many constructors and finitely many base-field elements. The topological existence argument used to choose auxiliary algebraic data is not an instruction to carry out infinitely many approximating constructions. It establishes that finite suitable data exist. This is sufficient for constructibility; an efficient uniform recipe is a separate deliverable.

## 8. Checks performed and limits

The geometry audit inspected all eight constructors and both geometric reconstruction paths. Eighteen exact rational-polynomial coefficient checks, using a standard-library sparse-polynomial checker separately from Lean, passed. They check the sextic expansion, reconstruction of m,q,A and radius, the norm and circle identities, the line and collinearity identities, the unit distance, signed recovery, f(1), and the cubic identities and recovery formulas. These checks use symbolic coefficients, not sampled floating-point placements. The rational identities are interpreted under the nonzero-denominator hypotheses established above. They supplement the written proof and are not another trusted proof kernel.

The Lean source was not changed or recompiled in this audit. The previously accepted theorem and its reported dependency list remain the formal basis: `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`, with no `sorryAx`.

No geometric soundness gap was found. The bridge implication in §2 now has a complete written proof, so the formal theorem has the advertised geometric interpretation for standard relative RMC constructibility.

The separate claim in the earlier manuscript of **one final line–circle verging operation** is not certified by this audit or by the final Lean theorem. The checked recovery uses the adjacent-product values beta_1 and beta_3 to recover g_0=P/(beta_1 beta_3), and then the quintic generator. A separate argument is needed to replace that recovery with the manuscript's stronger one-operation claim. This does not obstruct finite constructibility.

Likewise, this report does not establish priority, peer-review acceptance, an optimized operation count, a complete worked conductor-25 construction, or general-quintic solvability.

## 9. Result available for the paper

Combining the checked theorem with the bridge proved here gives:

> Let K be a subfield of the real numbers and L/K a Galois extension of degree five embedded in the real numbers. Every element of L is constructible relative to K by finitely many ordinary straightedge-and-compass operations and unit-marked-ruler vergings between a line and a circle through an already constructed point.

This applies to every irreducible cyclic quintic over a real coefficient field. To see the real-root condition, complex conjugation on its splitting field has order dividing two, whereas the Galois group C5 has no nontrivial element of order two. Consequently conjugation is trivial and every root is real. Taking K to be the field generated by the supplied coefficients shows that the construction starts from those coefficients and the unit segment. All five roots lie in L and are covered by the theorem.

**Disposition:** geometry correspondence passed; no Lean repair is required for the finite-constructibility conclusion. The next manuscript revision should incorporate this bridge, state existential relative constructibility explicitly, and distinguish the stronger operation-count claim from the checked central theorem.
